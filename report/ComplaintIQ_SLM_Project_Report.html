1Executive summary

ComplaintIQ reads a customer's complaint narrative and predicts which financial product it concerns and what the issue is, choosing from nine supported product/issue pairs, or flags it as out of scope. A reviewer confirms or corrects every prediction in a desktop web application; nothing is sent to an external AI service.

What was built

  • Two fine-tuning rounds. Phase 1 fine-tuned Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct on 9,000 historical complaints in a cloud notebook and ran it on a MacBook Air M1. Phase 2 fine-tuned the larger Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 on a 70,162-complaint, time-split dataset entirely on a local Windows PC, and it is now the deployed model.
  • A complete review application: React/TypeScript interface, FastAPI service, SQLite case store with append-only history, durable processing queue, CSV import, backups and an evaluation page, all bound to 127.0.0.1.
  • Measured evidence for every claim: each figure in this report is read from a saved evaluation or log file in the project.

Key results

  • On the project's original 153 reserved test complaints, the deployed Qwen3 model reaches 90.85% product and 58.82% joint product/issue accuracy, against 88.89% / 54.25% for the Phase 1 Qwen2.5 model and 88.24% / 55.56% for the TF-IDF baseline, and answers about 4.7x faster than Phase 1 did on the Mac (204 ms vs 953 ms, different hardware).
  • The model never produced an invalid label (0 invalid replies in 3,000 test cases), because decoding is constrained to the allowed answers.
  • Out-of-scope detection: precision 82.3% and recall 65.3% on the test set; 88.9% of hand-written unrelated texts were flagged.

Honest limits

  • The three credit-reporting issues are the hardest classes (F1 about 48-50%): their narratives overlap heavily.
  • Compressing the model to 4-bit GGUF for fast local serving cost accuracy: on 300 identical test rows the GGUF scored 59.7% vs 64.7% for the unquantized fine-tuned model.
  • To fit a three-hour window, training stopped after 950 steps (about 22% of one epoch). A full epoch may score differently.

2Problem, scope and approach

Financial institutions receive large volumes of free-text complaints. Routing each one to the right team depends on understanding which product it concerns and what went wrong. The assignment was to build a working complaint-intelligence pilot around a small language model that is fine-tuned on the task and runs locally, measured honestly against a traditional baseline.

Label space

Three product families and nine issue pairs, chosen from the public CFPB Consumer Complaint Database where narratives are plentiful, plus one out-of-scope class added in Phase 2 so the model itself can decline unrelated text.

Approach

  1. Baseline first. A TF-IDF + logistic-regression classifier sets the bar (product 88.24%, joint 55.56% on 153 reserved complaints).
  2. Choose a model that fits the hardware. Candidates were benchmarked locally before any training (Section 6).
  3. Fine-tune with QLoRA (4-bit base model + small trainable LoRA adapters), so training fits a single consumer GPU.
  4. Generative classifier with constrained decoding. The model answers with the application's exact JSON ({"product": ..., "issue": ...}), and decoding is restricted to the ten valid answers, so every reply is a legal label.
  5. Export to 4-bit GGUF and serve it with llama.cpp behind the application; a human confirms every decision.

Priority, department routing and suggested next steps are explicit, documented rules, not model predictions. Summaries are extractive previews. No confidence or risk percentages are invented.

3System architecture

Request flow

  1. A complaint is submitted (single narrative or CSV of up to 100 rows / 2 MB). It is written to the durable queue before inference.
  2. The scope gate checks for card, credit-report or bank-account context and runs a TF-IDF classifier trained on supported vs other-product complaints; out-of-scope text goes to review without calling the model.
  3. The first 2,000 normalized characters go to llama-server with the system prompt and a JSON schema that only allows the valid product/issue pairs. The reply is validated again in Python.
  4. Rules add priority, department and a suggested next step; the case is saved with its original prediction, which is never overwritten.
  5. A reviewer confirms or corrects the labels (a reason is required), adds notes and moves the case through Queued → In review → Resolved. Every change is appended to the case history.

Halving the per-step batch and doubling gradient accumulation keeps the optimisation identical (16 sequences per update) while stopping memory from spilling out of the 12 GB card. That made each step about a third faster. The step count was then chosen so that training plus evaluation and export would finish in about three hours.

10Application and quality assurance

Features

  • Cases: searchable, filterable queue (product, priority, department, dates, review state), URL-backed filters and CSV export, including an export of human-confirmed labels.
  • Case detail: full narrative, the immutable original prediction and policy assessment, review form (corrections need a reason), notes, lifecycle transitions and an append-only history.
  • Analyze and Imports: single complaints or CSV batches go through one durable queue that survives restarts; failed rows can be retried and pending ones cancelled.
  • Model & System: the loaded model and its training data size (read from the server), measured evaluation with limitations, storage and queue health, one-click backups.
  • Safety: loopback-only binding, same-origin writes, revision checks against stale edits, no silent fallback to another model.

11Limitations

  • Labels are consumer-selected. CFPB product and issue fields are chosen by the person filing, so some "errors" are label noise.
  • Partial epoch. The deployed model saw 15,200 of 70,162 training complaints. It was a deliberate time trade-off; a full epoch (about 9.3 h at the measured speed) was not run.
  • Quantization loss. The 4-bit GGUF trails the unquantized adapter by about 5 points on 300 rows.
  • Fixed taxonomy. Three products and nine issues; everything else is "out of scope", not classified.
  • Truncation. Only the first 2,000 characters are classified, and 25% of training complaints were shortened to 512 tokens.
  • Single operator. No authentication or multi-user permissions; the pilot is meant for one reviewer on one machine.
  • Rules are rules. Priority and routing come from documented phrase rules, not a trained severity model.
